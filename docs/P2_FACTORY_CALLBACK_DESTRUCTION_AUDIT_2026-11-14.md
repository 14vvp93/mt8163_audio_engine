## Factory destructor

The vtable relocation inventory identifies:

- non-deleting `AudioALSAHardware::~AudioALSAHardware()` at `0x000c56e4`, size 400 bytes;
- deleting destructor thunk at `0x000c5874`, size 16 bytes.

The non-deleting destructor body at `0x000c56e4` performs the following relevant operations:

1. Rewrites the object's vptr to the base/destruction vtable.
2. Loads the member at `this+0x04`; if non-null, calls its virtual function at vtable byte offset `+0x04`. The callee's semantics are not identified by this audit.
3. Releases the allocation referenced at `this+0x39c`, if non-null.
4. Clears several global pointer locations.
5. Runs `VectorImpl::finish_vector()` and `VectorImpl::~VectorImpl()` on the callback vector beginning at `this+0x3b0`.
6. Rewrites the lock member at `this+0x3ac` to its destruction vtable and calls `alock_free()`.
7. Finishes and destroys a separate sorted vector at `this+0x384`.

No direct call to `clearAudioParameterChangedCallback`, no callback-entry iteration/removal loop, and no explicit in-flight callback wait/drain is visible in this destructor body. The callback vector is destroyed before the per-device lock is freed.

**Boundary:** the virtual cleanup call through `this+0x04` and the legacy device `close` path may have additional behavior not resolved here. This destructor disassembly alone cannot prove that callbacks remain admitted during destruction, nor can it prove that an earlier close operation has drained them. It does show that the destructor body itself does not visibly perform an explicit callback-registry clear/drain protocol before destroying the vector and lock.

## Registration and clear

`AudioALSAHardware::setAudioParameterChangedCallback(callback, cookie)` at `0x000ce9dc`:

- uses the lock at `this+0x3ac`;
- operates on the callback vector at `this+0x3b0`, with size at `this+0x3b8`;
- compares both callback pointer and cookie;
- allocates an 8-byte pair containing those two values and inserts its pointer into the vector.

`AudioALSAHardware::clearAudioParameterChangedCallback(cookie)` at `0x000cecc0`:

- uses the same lock and vector;
- matches entries by cookie;
- removes matching pointer slots using `VectorImpl::removeItemsAt`;
- unlocks and returns.

No direct deallocation of the separately allocated 8-byte pair is visible in the clear method's disassembly. Whether another ownership layer frees those records is unresolved; do not promote this observation to a confirmed leak without inspecting the relevant Vector/allocator contracts and all paths.

## Dispatch and lock-failure behavior

`AudioALSAHardware::onAudioParameterChangedCallback(char const*)` at `0x000cef18`:

- acquires the same lock;
- traverses the callback vector;
- loads each callback pointer and cookie;
- invokes the callback indirectly **while the registry lock is held**;
- unlocks only after the loop.

In registration, clear, and dispatch, a nonzero result from `alock_lock_ms` enters a warning path and then falls through into the vector operation. None of these methods aborts the operation on lock-acquisition failure. Therefore:

- successful lock acquisition serializes clear against dispatch;
- lock-acquisition failure permits mutation/traversal/callback invocation without the lock;
- clear is not an unconditional in-flight drain barrier;
- a synchronous reentrant callback can contend for the registry lock while dispatch is waiting for that callback to return.

This is static evidence of a race/deadlock-capable design, not proof that a runtime race, use-after-free, or deadlock has occurred.

## P2 implications

The current factory implementation does not establish the required callback-lifetime contract. The replacement must:

1. close callback admission before unregister/destruction;
2. take an in-flight reference before dispatch;
3. invoke callbacks outside the registry mutation lock;
4. remove registrations atomically with admission closure;
5. wait for all in-flight references to drain before releasing the cookie owner or registry storage;
6. fail closed when synchronization cannot be acquired;
7. define self-unregister and cross-process reentrancy behavior;
8. make callback-entry ownership explicit and testable.

The target HIDL destructor calls the legacy device `close` function before releasing its callback strong-reference member, but the close callback implementation and its callback-drain semantics remain unresolved. The factory destructor's virtual cleanup call through `this+0x04` also remains unresolved.

## Follow-up: callback registry lock-failure and dispatch-order regression gates

The pinned factory ELF report from workflow run [43585742590](https://github.com/14vvp93/mt8163_audio_engine/actions/runs/43585742590), artifact ID `14110606564`, confirms three additional fail-open paths:

- Registration: `setAudioParameterChangedCallback` calls `alock_lock_ms@plt` at `0xcea38`; nonzero status logs and falls through to registry/vector access at `0xcea9c`.
- Clear: `clearAudioParameterChangedCallback` calls `alock_lock_ms@plt` at `0xced08`; nonzero status logs and falls through to registry/vector access at `0xced6c`.
- Dispatch: `onAudioParameterChangedCallback` calls `alock_lock_ms@plt` at `0xcef60`; nonzero status logs and falls through to registry traversal at `0xcefc4`.

The dispatch loop invokes the callback indirectly at `0xcf020..0xcf024` before calling `alock_unlock@plt` at `0xcf050`. Thus callbacks execute while the registry lock is held on the lock-success path. This can create a reentrancy/lock-cycle hazard; actual deadlock is not established.

Audit-script commits `29bb71bf04239634d4e955e40536de955111db23` and `855e5a5b205594bc69b22ddd93dcbf406b864094` add exact regression assertions for all three lock-failure fallthroughs and callback-before-unlock ordering. These new assertions are not verified until CI for the latest commit passes and its artifact is inspected.

The five lock-failure surfaces now targeted by the audit are the two singleton accessors plus callback registration, clear, and dispatch. They must not be treated as safe serialization. P2 remains OPEN; Primary HAL remains fail-closed and `installable: false`.


## Follow-up: resolve AudioALSAHardware+0x04 teardown dispatch

The factory constructor disassembly at `0x000c5388..0x000c538c` calls `AudioALSAStreamManager::getInstance()` and stores the returned pointer at `AudioALSAHardware+0x04`.

The factory vtable relocation report for `_ZTVN7android22AudioALSAStreamManagerE` identifies:
- complete destructor slot `+0x008 -> 0x00077b58`;
- deleting destructor slot `+0x00c -> 0x00077e20`.

The `AudioALSAHardware` destructor at `0x000c5720..0x000c5734` loads `this+0x04`, reads its vptr, loads vtable byte offset `+0x04`, and invokes it. Therefore the previously unresolved virtual cleanup call is statically resolved:

`AudioALSAHardware::~AudioALSAHardware() -> AudioALSAStreamManager deleting destructor(0x00077e20)`.

The StreamManager destructor at `0x00077b58` clears its own singleton pointer and calls `AudioEventThreadManager::getInstance()->unregisterCallback(0)`, then destroys its own vectors and synchronization objects. This is a separate manager from `AudioALSAHardware`'s parameter-change callback registry (`this+0x3ac` lock and `this+0x3b0` vector). The observed call does not directly invoke `AudioALSAHardware::clearAudioParameterChangedCallback` and does not establish that the parameter-change callback registry is drained. The internal semantics of `AudioEventThreadManager::unregisterCallback(0)` are outside the pinned factory ELF and remain unknown.

The P2 device-close contract audit now correlates the constructor store, destructor virtual dispatch, and StreamManager vtable relocation. CI must pass before this new regression gate is treated as verified.

### Revised teardown interpretation

The factory teardown order is now statically identified as:
1. `HMI.close` invokes the deleting destructor of `AudioALSAHardware`.
2. `AudioALSAHardware` invokes the deleting destructor of its `AudioALSAStreamManager` member at `+0x04`.
3. `AudioALSAStreamManager` unregisters `AudioEventType::0` from `AudioEventThreadManager` and destroys its own state.
4. `AudioALSAHardware` proceeds to destroy the parameter-change callback vector and free its lock.

No proof yet connects step 3 to a drain of the separate parameter-change callback registry in step 4. Keep that as an open contract rather than assuming the manager cleanup is irrelevant or sufficient.

## Follow-up: AudioEventThread type-0 unregister is a stop-and-join barrier

Evidence: workflow run [43588457542](https://github.com/14vvp93/mt8163_audio_engine/actions/runs/43588457542), audited code commit `e8721fc04e5489de1b4c224359e44d98a89b5e17`, artifact ID `14111186560`, artifact ZIP SHA-256 `bed55851b8da6a7d57e02ffc2f760412b22211931d7392e62497b6695a30f88e`. Reviewed report: `p2-factory-resource-manager-lifecycle.txt`. Factory ELF SHA-256: `112744a4dad30d809d638d8aecd75ddba1ed7bbfcda8c4d2f9fde4463d8bfde4`.

The StreamManager constructor at `0x00076d94` registers an `AudioEventThreadManager` callback for event type `0`, passing the StreamManager instance as the callback cookie (`0x7721c..0x77230`). The StreamManager destructor at `0x00077b58` calls `AudioEventThreadManager::unregisterCallback(0)` before destroying its own vectors and locks.

The one-argument `unregisterCallback(AudioEventType)` at `0x0013612c` finds the worker for the requested type, writes zero to its run flag at worker offset `+0x1c`, signals the worker, calls `pthread_join(worker->thread_id, nullptr)`, and only then removes the worker pointer from the manager's vector. The worker loop at `0x00135034` checks this run flag around its condition wait and callback-dispatch loop and exits via `pthread_exit()`. The worker destructor at `0x001353b4` independently uses the same stop/signal/join sequence before destroying synchronization state.

This resolves the event-thread teardown semantics: type-0 unregister waits for that worker thread to exit. It is stronger than a callback-list removal and is a worker-level drain barrier for that worker.

It does **not** close the factory parameter-change callback lifetime gate. The event-thread worker is registered by `AudioALSAStreamManager` with a StreamManager cookie; the private HIDL parameter-change callback registry is a distinct `AudioALSAHardware` vector at `this+0x3b0`, with lock at `this+0x3ac`. The inspected disassembly does not prove that the event-thread callback dispatches through `AudioALSAHardware::onAudioParameterChangedCallback`, nor that stopping this worker prevents any other caller from entering that registry. The registry's lock-acquisition failure still falls through into vector access, and its callback invocation still occurs under the registry lock on the successful-lock path.
